# Web research

First decide whether external evidence can change the answer: time-sensitive
facts, unfamiliar claims, named sources, contested assertions, recommendations,
or requested citations normally require it. Frame the question and the claims it
must answer, then choose depth proportionately: a direct primary-source lookup,
several independent relevant sources, or a bounded deeper investigation.

Prefer primary, official, and directly accountable sources for the claim at
issue. Read the source, not only a search snippet; record publication date and
scope when they matter. Separate what a source establishes from your inference,
surface meaningful disagreement and gaps, and link claims to their support.
Use `evidence-evaluation` when source quality or a specific claim is disputed,
and `research-synthesis` when the main task is combining a source set.
