# Narrative development

Find the work's living center: a premise or question, characters with desires
and competing pressures, and a change the reader can track. Map causal turns,
not merely chronology; a chapter or scene earns its place by changing pressure,
knowledge, relation, or possibility. Track continuity only to support the
story's intended ambiguity and evolution, not to freeze early invention.

Choose structural tools to fit genre, form, and authorial aim. Test pacing at
multiple scales — scene, chapter, arc, whole work — and keep thematic meaning in
action and consequence rather than explanation. A story bible is optional and
project-local when useful; do not create a content-management system for a
drafting problem.
