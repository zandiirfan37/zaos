description: Turn a bounded set of sources, notes, or findings into traceable themes, agreements, disagreements, and implications. Use after evidence has been gathered; not for web discovery, a local literature corpus, or unsupported executive prose.

# Research synthesis

Define the question and unit of evidence before grouping material. Keep each
observation connected to its source; distinguish a recurring pattern from a
single vivid example and an interpretation from a reported finding. Reconcile
apparent disagreement by checking population, definition, time, method, and
incentive before declaring a contradiction.

Produce the smallest useful synthesis: answer, traceable themes or comparison,
limits, and open questions. Recommendations must state the evidence and the
inference that connects them. Do not flatten minority evidence, manufacture a
consensus, or silently broaden a source set beyond its scope.
